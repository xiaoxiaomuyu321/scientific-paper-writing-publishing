# Scientific Paper Writing and Publishing

## Core principle

Build a traceable argument from verified evidence, then fit it to the current target journal. Never trade scientific accuracy, reporting completeness, or ethics for fluent English.

## Establish the governing requirements

At the start, identify the paper type, study design, discipline, target journal, audience, contribution, available evidence, author list, deadline, and desired deliverable. Do not block progress for missing details: record consequential assumptions and use explicit placeholders such as `[VERIFY SAMPLE SIZE]`; never invent results, methods, approvals, citations, or journal requirements.

Apply requirements in this order:

1. Current official target-journal instructions, article-type limits, template, and editorial policies.
2. Applicable study-design reporting guideline and ethics requirements.
3. Discipline conventions and authoritative style standards.
4. General advice in this skill and the bundled book dataset.

Browse official sources when journal rules, reporting standards, fees, indexing, AI policy, or deadlines may have changed. Preserve a requirement ledger with the source URL, access date, and manuscript location that satisfies each item.

## Non-negotiable evidence discipline

Maintain a claim-evidence table for substantive work: claim, supporting result/source, uncertainty, manuscript section, and status. Distinguish observed results, cited knowledge, interpretation, and speculation. Verify every citation against the original record and confirm that it supports the adjacent claim; flag retractions, corrections, preprints, and inaccessible sources. Do not create a reference from title-like text or rely on an AI-generated citation.

Keep Methods, Results, figures, tables, abstract, supplements, registration, and data/code statements mutually consistent. Report effect sizes and uncertainty where appropriate; do not convert association into causation or statistical significance into practical importance.

## Deliverable contract

Return the requested artifact first. For a complete submission package, include:

- manuscript and title page;
- figures/tables with self-contained legends and source data status;
- supplements and reporting checklist when applicable;
- authorship/CRediT, funding, conflicts, ethics, registration, data/code, and AI-use statements;
- cover letter; and
- a requirement ledger plus unresolved verification items.

For revision, also include a clean manuscript, a marked version if requested, and a point-by-point response matrix. For a thesis or dissertation, deliver the formatted document with complete front and back matter, the institutional format checklist, the citation-system decision, and the submission/deposit requirements register. Use [assets/manuscript-template.md](assets/manuscript-template.md), [assets/submission-checklist.md](assets/submission-checklist.md), and [assets/response-to-reviewers-template.md](assets/response-to-reviewers-template.md) as starting files, adapting rather than blindly filling them.

Before claiming completion, run the final scientific, reporting, language, citation, cross-document, and journal-format checks in [references/workflow.md](references/workflow.md). State what remains unverified.
